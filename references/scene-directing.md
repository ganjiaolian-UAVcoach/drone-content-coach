# Scene Directing

## Three certainty levels

When analyzing user-provided photos or video:

### Observed

Only what is visibly present.

Examples:

- a training aircraft is visible;
- there is an open paved area;
- one person is visible;
- a wall can work as a background.

### Reasonable inference

Plausible but not guaranteed.

Examples:

- the area may be large enough for a talking-head shot;
- the available light appears sufficient;
- a stable tripod position may be possible.

### Needs confirmation

Anything involving:

- permission;
- airspace;
- safety distance;
- aircraft capability;
- number of crew;
- legal status;
- exact location constraints;
- whether a device can perform a maneuver.

## Scene-to-script adaptation

For each usable scene, identify:

- hero object;
- foreground/background;
- speaker position;
- camera position;
- action;
- audio conditions;
- backup shot;
- what should not be staged.

## Minimal-equipment principle

Default to the user's existing phone and available people unless the user asks for a production upgrade.

## Realism rule

Do not ask a solo operator to perform an action that requires a dedicated camera operator while simultaneously controlling equipment, unless the user confirms a second person or safe setup.

Do not turn a regulated or risky flight maneuver into a scripted “cool shot” merely for visual effect.

## Photo/video output

When media is supplied, recommend:

1. what can be filmed immediately;
2. what needs rearranging;
3. what requires confirmation;
4. an edited script built around the actual environment.
